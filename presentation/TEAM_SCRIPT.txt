SAFE JUDGE ANSWERS

Is this real site data or a live installation?
No. This is a working software prototype using synthetic data. The presentation illustrates saved model results. Customers and routes are candidates. We do not claim site measurements, signed customers, an installed network, or live equipment control.

Did machine learning improve profits?
No. Forecast-controlled operating margin was about 2.43% lower in New York and 2.31% lower in Lansing than the rule controller, over 504 test hours per site. Better forecasting alone did not improve this decision result.

What do the confidence numbers mean?
Facility mean absolute error was 22.1% lower in New York and 20.6% lower in Lansing than the best simple test baseline. Nominal 90% facility intervals covered about 92.3% and 90.6% of synthetic holdout values. Coverage is measured, not guaranteed.

What do the physical checks prove?
Across three controllers, two sites, and 504 hours per site, 3,024 executed controller-hours had zero checked physical constraint violations. These are simulation checks, not equipment certification. The assumed 2.5 MW is source thermal recovery; heat-pump output adds compressor electricity.

Why choose New York, and are the savings guaranteed?
The separate annual model assumes a compact urban network and larger heat demand. Useful heat is about 12.5 versus 5.24 GWh/year; capital is $9.1M versus $11.7M. Net present values are +$0.90M and -$12.52M over 25 years at 7%. These depend on unvalidated assumptions.

Who owns it, and how does the community benefit?
A proposed network company owns the network and carries investment risk. The data center retains cooling responsibility. New York models about $25,000/year for a community fund. Tenant savings require enforceable pass-through. Estimated net operational emissions avoided are about 2,127 versus 841 tonnes/year. These are conditional annual estimates.
